## [2026-09-22] ingest | does stability compose? — two isolations, neither of which can ask

Up: [dev](../dev.md) · [testing](../testing/testing.md) §Replay scenario C · record
`docs/design/adaptive-stability.md` §5 · code `src/control/scenario_c.rs`.

**The claim under test.** The adaptive-stability ADR's sharper sentence: *loops that oscillate
together while each is stable alone.* Scenario C's witness removes every breaker at once, which
shows that safety devices are load-bearing — a weaker and less interesting statement, and the wiki
already recorded it as **not shown**.

This entry is what happened when someone tried, so the next attempt begins after it rather than
repeating it. **Nothing shipped.** The experiment's code was reverted; only the knowledge is kept,
because a mechanism that cannot answer the question it was built for is worse than none — it looks
like coverage.

### What has to be decided to ask the question at all

Running "one loop alone" means deciding what the other loops do meanwhile. The three governors in
this plant — opacity, tuning, membership — all act on **one shared state variable**, `fill`. That
turns out to be the whole difficulty.

### Method 1 — freeze the other actuators at their initial values

Cut the coupling: the governor still runs and still decides, its decision just does not reach
`fill`.

**It does not isolate a loop. It removes the plant's means of relief.** With opacity and the writer
knob frozen, `drain` is pinned at `1024/8192 = 0.125` while `share` can reach `0.35`, so `fill`
saturates at 1.0 and stays there. Measured: **24 of 96 schedules "violate"** under *membership
alone*, all `NotAtRest`.

That number is an **uncontrollable plant, not an unstable loop**, and it is exactly the shape of
result that is tempting to report as the ADR's sentence demonstrated. It would have been a
fabrication: the loop under test is not what diverged.

### Method 2 — drive the other actuators from the coupled run's trace

Better shape. The plant stays controllable, every exogenous condition is identical, and the only
difference is which feedback path is live — which is what *"stable alone"* has to mean for loops
sharing a plant.

**But the reference carries the coupled dynamics into the isolated run.** Measured with the
breakers off: **24 of 96 for every configuration alike** — one loop or three, with *identical*
violation counts (`KnobChatter` 80, `ReleaseFlap` 16). The chatter is imported from the trace, not
generated by the loop under test. The method cannot distinguish the two things it exists to
separate.

### What was established

With the shipped breakers on, every configuration is quiet: **0 of 96 schedules × 2 enforcing
profiles**, coupled or isolated. So the harness establishes that *the shipped breakers hold across
this sweep*. It does not establish whether they are **needed because of coupling** — which is the
ADR's actual claim.

### Why this is structural

The obstacle is not a missing afternoon. **The loops share one state variable**, so any way of
holding "the others" fixed is either uncontrollable (method 1) or is the coupled run wearing a
disguise (method 2). Demonstrating the ADR's sentence needs a plant where the loops act on
*different* variables that interact — which this one is not.

### The sentence to keep

**A negative result is only worth having if it says what was ruled out.** "Not shown" invites the
next person to try the obvious thing; *"not shown, and here are two methods that cannot show it,
with the reason each fails"* is the part that compounds.
